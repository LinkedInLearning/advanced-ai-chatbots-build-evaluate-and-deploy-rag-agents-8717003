# Roya's Imaginary Cafe — About Us

## Our Story

Roya's Imaginary Cafe began in 2017 as a single espresso cart outside a downtown
Cambridge marketplace, run by our founder out of a converted bicycle trailer. The
line got long enough, often enough, that a year later we opened a proper café a
few blocks away. We grew the way we'd want any small business to grow — slowly,
and only when we could keep the quality up. Today we're six neighborhood cafés
and a working roastery, still in and around Cambridge.

## Mission and Values

Great coffee, zero pretension, and a job people don't dread. We think specialty
coffee got a little too precious somewhere along the way, and we'd rather make an
excellent cup feel welcoming than impressive. Three things guide our calls:

- **Sourcing we can stand behind.** We buy direct, pay above commodity, and tell
  you where the beans came from.
- **People over throughput.** A café is a workplace first. If a policy is good
  for the spreadsheet but bad for the team, it's a bad policy.
- **Part of the block.** Each café should feel like it belongs to its
  neighborhood, not like a logo dropped onto a corner.

## Sustainability

We roast in small batches to cut waste, compost our grounds and pastry trim, and
send unsold pastries to a local community fridge at the end of each day. Our bean
bags are compostable kraft, and we offer a 25-cent discount for bringing a clean
reusable cup. Our remaining single-use cold cups are plastic; we're piloting
compostable replacements at the Cambridge café. The Cambridge roastery runs on a
rooftop solar array that covers a meaningful share of its daytime load.

## In the Community

Every café hosts a monthly open-mic night and rotates work from local artists on
its walls — the art is for sale, and we take no cut. We donate coffee to
neighborhood mutual-aid groups and run a "suspended coffee" board where guests can
pre-pay a drink for someone who needs one. Twice a year we host free public
cupping sessions to demystify how coffee is tasted and graded.

## Press and Recognition

We've been written up in a couple of local "best coffee" roundups and won a
regional roasters' guild award for our Ethiopia Yirgacheffe. We're proud of those,
but the review we care about most is whether the same faces come back the next
morning.

## Working Here

We hire for warmth first and train the coffee. Open roles are posted on our
website; pay, benefits, and how we work together are spelled out in our employee
handbook. If you've ever wanted to learn to roast, ask — most of our roasters
started on the bar.
